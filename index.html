Frequently Asked Questions

How do you calculate Pressure Ratio on a turbo compressor map?
**Pressure Ratio (PR) = (Atmospheric Pressure + Boost PSI) / Atmospheric Pressure**. At sea level (where atmospheric pressure is 14.7 PSI), running 20 PSI of boost equals: $(14.7 + 20) / 14.7 = 2.36$. You locate 2.36 on the vertical Y-axis of a turbo compressor map to see if the compressor wheel operates in its maximum 75% efficiency island.
What is the rule of thumb converting turbo mass flow (lbs/min) to horsepower?
A standard internal combustion gasoline engine requires approximately 1 lb/min of airflow to produce **10 to 11 flywheel horsepower**. If your turbo compressor flows 50 lbs/min of air, your engine has the theoretical airflow capacity to make roughly 500 to 550 horsepower with proper fuel and tuning.
What happens if a turbocharger is sized too large or too small?
A turbo that is too large suffers from extreme **turbo lag** (it takes until 4,500+ RPM to generate boost, making the car feel sluggish around town). A turbo that is too small spools instantly off-idle, but chokes at high RPM ('runs out of breath') and blows superheated air, which can cause severe engine detonation and head gasket failure.
